Coral survey datasets contain correlated environmental measurements that can obscure the patterns associated with bleaching severity. This study asks whether principal component analysis (PCA) can provide an interpretable summary of those patterns. The analysis used 23,069 sampling events from 1998–2019 and 35 environmental inputs. Sampling events were consolidated, remaining input gaps were median-imputed, and selected power transformations and standardisation preceded PCA. Parallel analysis guided component retention; bleaching-group contrasts, source stratification and sensitivity checks supported interpretation. The 7 retained components explained 75.9% of environmental variance. The strongest pooled High-versus-Low score differences were PC4: δ = +0.47; PC3: δ = +0.44. The components summarise environmental variation but do not fully preserve observed bleaching-related separation. Original indicators should therefore be retained alongside PCA summaries, and associations should be validated across independent sources and periods before operational use.
